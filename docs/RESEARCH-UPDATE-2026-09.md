# Research update: small-budget hotel advertising

Reviewed September 9, 2026. This is a public operating update, not a report of changes made in any live account. The priority is qualified reservation demand within the owner's spending boundary. More impressions alone do not establish progress.

## Decisions supported by the research

| Priority | What to do | Why / boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Validate booking and qualified-call measurement | Otherwise automation can learn to maximize cheap engagement instead of reservations |
| 2 | Reconcile billing mode, budget history and combined hotel spend | A campaign's daily limit is not automatically an account-wide limit |
| 3 | Use focused Search as a controlled starting hypothesis | Small budgets leave little room for unrelated queries; this is not proof that PMax never works |
| 4 | Verify free booking links and mobile rate/availability accuracy | The direct booking route can improve without additional paid-media spend |
| 5 | Improve genuine image variety and investigate real search terms | Correct property details matter more than maximizing an optimization score |
| 6 | Evaluate over a meaningful sample | One booking or a few clicks cannot prove a winning strategy |

## Measurement traps to prevent

A local call-button tap, a connected call, a qualified reservation inquiry and a confirmed booking are different events. Local “Clicks to call” is biddable in PMax, while Search requires an appropriate call-conversion action for that objective. Direction requests can be attributed after a later organic interaction. Report these separately. [Local actions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9013908?hl=en)

Primary/secondary labels are not the whole configuration: a secondary action included in a custom goal still participates in bidding. Inspect campaign goal membership before describing an action as observation-only. [Conversion actions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11461796?hl=en)

Hotel Center's property-details **estimated booking value uses an assumed 5% click-to-conversion rate**. It is not measured booking revenue and must not be copied into a client report as confirmed sales. Use booking-provider confirmations and reconciled revenue instead. [Property preview and estimate](https://support.google.com/hotelprices/answer/9144476?hl=en)

## Budget and bid controls

For most average-daily-budget campaigns, `$2.50` average corresponds to a `$5` daily billed-ad-cost limit. Two independent campaigns at that setting can therefore reach `$10` combined in a day. Allocate the ceiling across every active campaign for the same hotel. Taxes, fees and prior budget changes require separate reconciliation. [Budget overview](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486536?hl=en)

“Pay for Conversions” billing has no daily limit; this is different from selecting a Maximize Conversions bid strategy. Campaign total budgets also lack a daily cap. The highest average budget selected during a day governs that day's limit. [Spending exceptions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486637?hl=en), [budget changes](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10487143?hl=en)

Use the billing view appropriate to the payment setup. Google's billed-cost report instructions apply to monthly invoicing; automatic payments and prepayments use Billing activity guidance. Compare charges and adjustments rather than assuming a campaign dashboard is the final invoice. [Daily-cost reporting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9237921?hl=en)

A Maximize Clicks CPC limit can have positive bid adjustments applied on top. Audit those adjustments before promising an absolute per-click maximum. Too low a bid can suppress delivery; distinguish low bids from low search demand before widening targeting. [Maximize Clicks](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268626?hl=en)

## Search intent, brands and PMax

Use hotel-name and destination intent carefully. Brand defense is conditional: inspect existing chain/OTA coverage, direct-rate competitiveness and the route to booking first. Strong reported brand conversion rates do not by themselves establish incremental bookings. Do not automatically buy every brand query or exclude every brand query; record the reason for the chosen approach.

PMax search themes are signals rather than a literal keyword allowlist. Campaign-level negatives are available, but apply to Search/Shopping inventory. Google's Hotel inventory in travel PMax does not support those negatives or brand exclusions. Account-wide negatives can inadvertently block another hotel. [Search themes](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16669486?hl=en), [control scope](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13607727?hl=en)

Brand exclusions can cover variants and related subsidiary brands. Review the scope before excluding a chain's parent brand. Same-domain keywords within an account are prioritized rather than simply bidding against one another, but correct property routing and budget allocation still matter. [Brand exclusions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16669487?hl=en), [keyword priority](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2756257?hl=en)

Do not restrict all future guests to people physically near the hotel. Choose supported traveler-origin markets and hotel/destination queries. Presence-only targeting in those markets is a controlled hypothesis; Google's travel guidance also recognizes destination interest. Use actual origin evidence to refine it. [Location options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038?hl=en)

## Photos and free booking links

Google recommends at least four **unique** relevant Search images and both square and landscape formats. Two photos cropped twice are four assets but not four distinct photographs. Favor actual current rooms, exterior and verified amenities; inspect every crop and preserve rights information. No image guarantees a conversion or that Google will show it. [Search image guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9566341?hl=en)

Free booking-link clicks do not appear in Google Ads and do not use its tracking template. Ask the authorized Hotel Center/booking provider to distinguish paid and free traffic in its reporting, excluding validation traffic. Hotel Center reporting can lag by two to three days. Do not label an empty Ads report as proof that free links generated no traffic. [Free-link tracking](https://support.google.com/hotelprices/answer/10473662?hl=en), [booking-link reports](https://support.google.com/hotelprices/answer/10474165?hl=en)

## How to learn without overclaiming

Google's general recommendation of at least 30 conversions over a longer evaluation period is not a universal Smart Bidding activation requirement. Strategy-specific eligibility still applies. At a tiny budget, validate tracking and correct clearly irrelevant traffic promptly, but avoid declaring success/failure after a fixed seven-day window. [Smart Bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882?hl=en)

For formal bidding experiments, Google recommends enough volume for at least 30 conversions in the relevant campaign/split and changing one variable at a time. Dividing a tiny budget can make a test less informative. Treat sequential changes as observational because seasonality, rates and demand may also change. [Experiment guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167141?hl=en)

Google's PMax store-goal guide recommends an average daily budget at least three times selected-action CPA. This is guidance, not a hard eligibility minimum. Cheap directions may satisfy that economics while yielding few bookings; use business outcomes to judge fit. [PMax budget guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15865049?hl=en)
